# LibGale — Story Notes

> Spoilers for the currently playable Chapter One.

## Premise

The library is a waypoint between dimensions. Its books are not objects; they
are living records of meaning. A book that is not read, understood or remembered
begins to starve for **meaningful energy**. It starts feeding on **meaningless
energy** instead: static, noise, forgotten words, the silence between shelves.

Enough meaningless energy turns a book into a **Rage Book / Hırs Kitabı**. It
does not want to hurt anyone. It wants to be understood. The librarian's job is
to enter the book, finish its story and seal it until a reader arrives.

## Whesper

The protagonist is a spirit with no body, no shadow and no memory of a former
life. The shelves whisper a name: **Whesper**. The player may write a different
name, but the default and the name the library uses is Whesper.

Whesper does not know whether they have a vessel. They know they have a purpose.
That is enough to start.

## The first three books

These three are not the library's only Rage Books. The Sealed Archive behind the
door holds far more than the hub shelves could ever carry. The three in the hall
were chosen as a **test**: the archive door's seal is bound to their meaning, so
a stranger cannot simply pull it open. Every Rage Book behind the door was
waiting for the same three words Whesper restores.

> TR: Bu üçü kütüphanenin tek Hırs Kitabı değil. Kapının ardındaki Yasak Arşiv,
> hub raflarının taşıyabileceğinden çok daha fazlasını barındırıyor. Salondaki
> üç kitap bir **sınav** olarak seçildi: arşiv kapısının mührü onların anlamına
> bağlı, bu yüzden yabancı biri kapıyı öylece çekip açamaz. Kapının ardındaki
> her Hırs Kitabı, Whesper'ın geri getirdiği aynı üç kelimeyi bekliyordu.

### 1. The Ashen Tale / Kül Masalı

A fairy tale that was read to a child every night, then shelved when the child
grew up. The book burned its own pages to feel warm again. Its missing meaning
is **Hope / Umut**. Its restored story remembers the child's voice and the
promise of one more page.

### 2. The Silent Poem / Sessiz Şiir

A librarian wrote a poem for a reader who came to the library every evening.
The reader stopped coming. The librarian tore out the last line so the poem
could never end—and perhaps the reader would have to return to finish it. Its
missing meaning is **Echo / Yankı**. Its restored story is answered by the
library itself.

### 3. The Last Page / Son Sayfa

The library's own final book. It contains the last page of every story and has
been refusing to end for a very long time, because an ending feels like being
forgotten. Its missing meaning is **End / Biter**. When Whesper writes it, the
library exhales. In the margin, Whesper's name appears in fresh ink.

## Chapter One ending

After the third seal, the archive door opens a crack. Margin says:

> The library knows every name written in its books. It does not know yours.

A voice from the dark answers:

> Then we will write it.

The screen reads **Chapter Two: The Unwritten**. That is the current stopping
point. The archive interior, the protagonist's full history and the finale are
not written yet.

## Themes

- A story needs a listener more than it needs an ending.
- Being forgotten is not the same as being ended.
- Meaning is a kind of gravity.
- The first three books are a tutorial arc, but they are also the first three
  pieces of Whesper's own story.

## Future notes (not implemented)
